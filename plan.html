The roadmap is a reference; this is a schedule. Each day is three to six hours: a set of topics, one thing you build with your hands, and a drill you answer out loud. The building and the speaking are not optional extras — they are the parts that convert recognition into competence.

How to run it

  • Read, then build, then speak. Reading alone produces the feeling of understanding and none of the substance. The build is short on purpose; the drill is where you find the gaps.
  • Do not skip week one because it looks familiar. Every AI topic in weeks two and three refers back to it — backpressure, idempotency, timeouts, percentiles, tenancy.
  • Tick a topic when you could explain it to someone else, including its failure modes — not when you have read it. A half-finished day honestly marked is worth more than a full one ticked out of momentum.
  • If you fall behind, drop the build before the drill. Speaking an answer is the highest-return thirty minutes in the plan.
  • Every day already includes a buffer of at least 45 minutes, and 35% on top of the focused time — for breaks, for re-reading the thing that did not land, and for the build that takes longer than it looks. The focused figure is the honest minimum; the planned figure is what to put in your calendar.

At the end of day fifteen, the output is not the ticks. It is the list of things you still could not explain, and a specific plan for each one. Part 18 of the roadmap has three longer routes depending on which of those you care about most.
